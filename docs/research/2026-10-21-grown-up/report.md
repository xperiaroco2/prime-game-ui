# A grown-up UI: what reads childish, what reads adult

Research for the engineer's verdict of 2026-10-21 (chat, translated): "the initial idea was cool, but now the interface
looks very childish, as if we were making a game for kids". Three gatherer agents read the web (text only) in the
workflow `wf_27131f48-f3e`; their findings are kept below as they returned them. The review page with the three
directions and the three icon styles is `pages/grownup/`. The evidence is practitioner writing and reviews, not
controlled studies: no source ties an outline weight or an icon style to a perceived age, so those cues are inference.

## Summary

| Cue | Childish | Grown-up | The sticker style today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Corner radius | large, pills everywhere | 0 to 8 px, few pills | 12 to 30 px, pills |
| Outline | a thick dark outline on every plate | none, or a thin rule; separation by flat colour blocks | 4 px ink on everything |
| Shadow | a big hard drop shadow | none, or one subtle edge | 5 px hard ink shadow |
| Display type | a bubbly rounded sans in Black for everything | one distinctive display face (heavy, condensed or angular; caps with open tracking) plus a quiet text sans | Nunito Black titles, Kablammo logo |
| Palette | many bright flat candy colours, pastel plates | few colours, a deep ground, one hot accent | deep purple plus bright mint plates, green, coral |
| Proportions | chunky controls, big padding | slimmer controls, tighter padding | chunky |
| Motion | bounce, squash | short hard cuts and slides | none yet |

The strongest levers, by the sources: radius, outline and shadow reduction, a restrained palette with one accent, and
condensed caps with open tracking. Rounded letterforms alone are a weak signal (the cues below); the childish read most
likely comes from the combination of thick outlines, hard shadows, big radii, Black weight everywhere and bright flat
plates. The hues are not the problem: every direction keeps deep purple, mint, the alien green and coral.

The critic's verdict on the drawn options (a ship bar, not a ranking): direction B, the flat broadcast look, is the only
one that clears it (grown-up 8, pretty 7.5 of 10); A, the sticker turned down, still reads as the sticker (5.5 / 6); C,
the alien tech, is clean but generic (7 / 6). Of the icons only the product-shot set reads grown-up (7 / 7), and it
still needs re-rolls; the vinyl-toy (4 / 5.5) and ink-line (3.5 / 5) sets do not.

## Cues: what reads childish or adult

- TYPE FAMILY. Direction: bubbly rounded sans for everything -> one distinctive display face for identity plus a plain, quiet sans for information. Why: children's apps use rounded playful sans for big headlines and interactive controls, and plainer faces for the grown-up reading text. A breakdown of Apex Legends describes the same split the other way round: an angular custom display face for identity, a clean condensed techno sans for HUD data (third-party font-ID blog, font names unconfirmed). ([source](https://www.uxmatters.com/mt/archives/2011/06/effective-use-of-typography-in-applications-for-children-3.php))
- TYPE FAMILY (apex display/info split, same cue, game side). Direction: single chunky voice -> layered system. Third-party description: heavy angular display face for logo and identity, cleaner condensed sans for dense HUD. Official fonts unconfirmed. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/apex-legends-font/))
- TYPE FAMILY, warning. Direction: overly curvy type -> tamer letterforms. A Discord community thread complained that the new rounded font had 'bizarre looking curves' and looked aimed at children for an age-restricted product. This is a user complaint, not a study, but it is the clearest 'rounded = childish' report I found. ([source](https://support.discord.com/hc/de/community/posts/1500001048362-Constructive-criticism-about-the-new-UI))
- TYPE FAMILY, counter-evidence. A dissertation slide reports that emotion responses to Helvetica Rounded Bold were not significantly different from Helvetica Bold. Implication: rounded terminals alone do not make a UI childish; swapping the font is not the main lever (matches the engineer's 'fonts are fine'). Secondary source, details unverified. Separately, I found no controlled study that tests 'rounded sans = childish' directly. ([source](https://www.slideshare.net/slideshow/koch-dissertation-defense-presentation-december-9-2011/11195390))
- TYPE WEIGHT. Direction: Black/ultra-heavy weight at every level -> heavy only on display words, regular or medium weight for labels and body. Why: the type-personality literature cites Poffenberger and Franken (1923): thin strokes read delicate and elegant, thick strokes strong and independent; a bold face carries 'heaviness and density'. So weight is a loudness dial, not by itself a kid marker (cited secondhand, hypothesis level). ([source](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2017.01229/full))
- TYPE WIDTH AND CASE. Direction: round mixed-case titles -> condensed, all-caps display set in a bold weight. Valorant's marketing-site breakdown (third-party analysis, not an official style guide): all-caps tightly tracked condensed display (Tungsten Bold), Inter 400 body, labels bold caps. Reads as confident, editorial and sharp. ([source](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/valorant/))
- TRACKING. Direction: tight default spacing on caps -> open tracking on caps labels. Butterick: add 5-12% extra letterspacing to all-caps or small-caps text, more at small sizes, never to lowercase. Valorant's breakdown uses 0.16em on buttons and 0.20em on labels (wider than Butterick, a stylised choice). Open-tracked caps is a standard 'premium, composed' signal (that last step is my inference, unconfirmed). ([source](https://practicaltypography.com/letterspacing.html))
- TYPE, GAME EXAMPLE. Direction: soft friendly face -> squared techno or classical-stylised faces. Cyberpunk 2077 UI is reported to be mostly Rajdhani (squared techno sans) with Orbitron secondary; Hades has a custom classical-edgy wordmark with clean body text (the Marcellus match is unconfirmed). Both are font-ID blogs, not studio statements. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=9721))
- TYPE, GAME EXAMPLE (Hades). Direction: decoration shouting -> ornament reserved for headers and names, clean readable body. Same source notes a review complaint that the font is too small: style should not cost legibility. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/hades-font/))
- CORNER RADIUS. Direction: large radius / pill shapes -> small radius or none. Hobday: rounding makes an interface feel softer, friendlier and less formal; a large radius is not needed for a soft impression; maximum rounding (full pill) is a stylistic choice; sharp corners are tied to seriousness. Also: keep rounding consistent. ([source](https://anthonyhobday.com/sideprojects/visualtechniques/techniques/rounded-corners/rounded-corners-as-stylistic-choice))
- CORNER RADIUS, GAME EXAMPLE. Direction: rounded plates -> square surfaces. Valorant's breakdown commits to 0px on buttons, cards and inputs; 4-6px only on tooltips and dropdowns; pills only for small badges. Material 3 also lets teams override its large-rounding default with smaller shapes, so radius is a brand dial. ([source](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/valorant/))
- OUTLINE WEIGHT AND COLOUR. Direction: thick near-black outline on every element (the 'sticker' look) -> hairline rules in a translucent tint of the text colour, with one accent line for emphasis. Neo-brutalist writing describes the thick 2-5px single flat-colour border as its signature (a graphic, playful look); Valorant's breakdown instead uses hairlines at ~15% opacity and a 2px red top stripe as the only signature. I found no study of outline weight vs age; the link between thick outline and 'kids' is inference (unconfirmed). ([source](https://medium.com/@designstudiouiux/neo-brutalism-web-design-what-it-is-why-it-works-and-when-to-use-it-f5d7932fa8ec))
- SHADOWS. Direction: 5px hard offset ink shadow on everything -> no shadow on most surfaces; elevation by rules, tone steps or a single soft shadow on modals only. Design guide: hard shadows are rarely used in modern interfaces because they look unrealistic and overly dramatic; useful only where stark separation is needed. Valorant's breakdown has no blurred shadow in the UI, one heavy 'cinematic' shadow on modals. A game-UI devlog adds that shadows add weight, and more professional UI tends to use softer ones. Note the Godot-safe limit: one shadow, no spread, no inset. ([source](https://cieden.com/book/sub-atomic/shadows/types-of-shadows))
- SHADOWS, GAME UI. Direction: heavy cartoon drop -> subtle shadow tuned to scene mood. Devlog advice: shadows make UI feel weightier; minimal professional UI uses softer shadows, dramatic scenes darker ones. Hobbyist devlog, low authority. ([source](https://itch.io/devlog/733325/ui-tips-for-game-updates.amp))
- PALETTE: ONE ACCENT. Direction: several saturated plates (purple, mint, green, coral all at full strength) -> deep neutral ground, off-white text and one signal accent. Persona 5's UI is built on black, white and red (Famitsu column by Sakurai); Valorant uses a near-black blue canvas (#0f1923), warm bone text (#ece8e1) and one signal red as the only chromatic accent. Adult-stylish games get 'attitude' from restraint plus one hot colour. ([source](https://sourcegaming.info/2016/11/24/the-aims-of-ui-sakurais-famitsu-column-vol-515/))
- PALETTE: SATURATION AND BRIGHTNESS. Direction: bright, pale, high-value fills -> darker values with saturation kept for accents. Valdez and Mehrabian (1994): brightness and saturation drive emotion strongly; Pleasure = .69B + .22S, Arousal = -.31B + .60S, Dominance = -.76B + .32S (signs differ in some secondary papers). Reading: darker colours raise a sense of dominance, saturation raises arousal. The 'adult' mapping is my inference (unconfirmed); and purple-blue, blue-green and purple ranked most pleasant, yellow least. ([source](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/7996122/))
- PASTEL VS DEEP. Direction: bright simple high-contrast fills (the kids'-game default) -> subdued, sophisticated palette, with vibrancy kept for interactive pieces. An itch.io palette guide says casual kids' games use bright simple colours while hardcore strategy uses subdued, sophisticated ones. A devlog notes pastels can still read dark and adult once desaturated and paired with earthy accents, so tone, not hue family, is what counts. ([source](https://itch.io/blog/1039646/picking-the-perfect-color-palette-for-your-game))
- PROPORTIONS. Direction: chunky oversized controls and icons with little text -> slimmer controls, tighter padding, more words, denser information. Children's-UX guides recommend 75x75px or larger targets (vs Apple's 44x44 for adults), big recognisable icons, little text for pre-readers, and louder, more vibrant feedback. Caveat: these are practitioner blogs, and a Finnish thesis found children and adults chose similar elements and colours, differing mostly in layout; a UBC study found icon guidelines are not age-specific. Treat size as one signal among several. Touch-target minimums on the phone still apply. ([source](https://www.aufaitux.com/blog/ui-ux-designing-for-children/))
- PROPORTIONS, COUNTERPOINT. Direction: layout matters more than colour: children and adults in a Finnish design thesis chose similar elements and colours, with the biggest differences in layout (so tighten layout rhythm, not just paint). ([source](https://www.utupub.fi/bitstream/10024/175685/1/Thesis-Mirva-Tapola.pdf))
- ICON STYLE. Direction: fat filled icons in coloured badges -> thinner line or mixed-weight glyphs. A design editorial says line icons feel lighter, more precise and premium-minimal; filled icons feel bolder and scan faster (opinion; a cited UNC master's paper claims filled icons are recognised faster, figures unverified). Because the game needs fast reading, filled for key actions and line for secondary is a sensible split (my suggestion). ([source](https://freeicon.com/blog/line-icons-vs-filled-icons-when-to-use-each))
- MOTION: BOUNCE. Direction: bouncy, elastic, overshoot easing on every button -> short, steep ease-out. web.dev: use bounce and elastic sparingly, 'if your project isn't intended to be fun, then don't have elements bouncing around the UI'; ease-outs sit around 200-500ms, bounces need 800-1200ms to settle. Design guides add that overshoot reads as playful character, and calm UIs use subtler curves. ([source](https://web.dev/articles/choosing-the-right-easing))
- MOTION: SNAP AND ATTITUDE. Direction: wobble and squash -> fast, hard-edged transitions with a stylised move. Valorant's breakdown: ~100ms hovers, ~420ms cinematic reveals, a sharp steep easing curve, primary buttons skewed -6 degrees, and opacity-only fallback under reduced motion. Persona 5: the selected item grows and changes colour, and portraits change pose per menu, so motion carries style without cuteness. ([source](https://www.webdesignhot.com/design.md/valorant/))
- ATTITUDE FROM SOURCES, NOT SOFTNESS. Direction: friendly mascot polish -> a punk/print or sports-broadcast reference. Persona 5's lead UI designer said the menus drew on 'the ransom-note-style covers of Sex Pistols albums', and the character designer cited the 'data-rich but often bombastic graphics of televised sports matches'; Atlus treats menus as a showpiece because players spend most time in them. A real-world graphic source gives crude, stylish, confident humour without cute shapes. ([source](https://www.dtnext.in/amp/story/edit/exuberant-video-game-menus-designed-with-da-vinci-in-mind-807823))
- CLUTTER AS STYLE. Direction: tidy bubble layout -> deliberately dense, overlapping, angled layouts, at the price of learnability. Sakurai's Famitsu column calls Persona 5's UI unconventionally cluttered and slow for newcomers, yet stylish. One design analysis credits size, colour and contrast, typography, alignment and proximity for keeping the busy look working. Suits a chaotic party game, but keep the core actions clear. ([source](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2022/04/23/visual-design-of-games-persona-5/))
- CONSISTENCY. Direction: random cheerful variety -> one visual language across every menu. A Persona 5 analysis credits the shared language of pause and battle menus for staying appealing over many hours; Hobday also advises consistent rounding. Applies to the new style: tokens should fix radius, outline and shadow once. ([source](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2022/04/23/visual-design-of-games-persona-5/))
- TEEN AUDIENCE. Direction: talking down -> simple but not childish. The Nielsen Norman teen study (38 teens, 23 sites, 2004) is quoted as: 'To work for teens, Web sites must be simple, but not childish, and supply plenty of interactive features'; teens leave from boredom or difficulty. Old (2004) and quoted secondhand by CNN. ([source](https://money.cnn.com/2005/01/31/technology/personaltech/Teenstudy/index.htm?cnn=yes))
- HUMOUR WITHOUT A KIDS' LOOK. Direction: cute shell -> cute or absurd content on a restrained, simple UI frame. Cult of the Lamb's art director said they added a darker layer partly because an earlier game was criticised as a kids' game, and the team said 'the cuter it is, the more we can push it'; a third-party review praised the UI's simplicity against the busy scene. Art-director quotes are from a search snippet (the page returned 404 on fetch), so treat as unverified. Takeaway: let the world carry the absurdity, and keep UI copy plain. ([source](https://www.dreadxp.com/?p=27832))
- RE-SKIN IS A STRONG LEVER. Direction: any. Sakurai (Smash dev video) argues that changing only the typefaces and palette of an ordinary options screen can shift how players perceive the whole game; he uses mock menus and Splatoon 3 as examples. Source is a news summary, not his words. ([source](https://gonintendo.com/contents/20898-masahiro-sakurai-stresses-the-importance-of-fonts-and-menu-design-in-latest-dev))
- COLOUR IS NOT THE PROBLEM. Direction: none needed. Harmonix first tried very clean, serene, respectful menus for a music game, then chose a much more colourful style (GDC 2010 report, Beatles Rock Band). Supports keeping the engineer's liked colours (deep purple, mint, alien green, coral) and changing shape, weight, shadow and type instead. ([source](https://www.engadget.com/2010-03-14-gdc-the-ui-of-the-beatles-rock-band.html))

The gatherer's note: Compact summary of how I read the evidence. Coverage is thin: none of the sources is a controlled study of game UI age-appeal. The type, radius and shadow cues rest on practitioner writing (Hobday, Butterick, cieden, web.dev), third-party design breakdowns (the Valorant one is a marketing-site analysis; the font-ID blogs are guesses) and Persona 5 interviews (Atlus designers quoted by the NYT via DTNext; Sakurai's Famitsu column). I found no source on outline weight or colour versus perceived age, on padding, or on icon size versus maturity. Those cues are inference and are marked (unconfirmed) where I made the leap. Best-supported levers: radius (0-4px), restrained palette with one accent, shadow and outline reduction, condensed caps with open tracking, and short snappy motion. One caution on the engineer's 'fonts are fine': the evidence says rounded terminals alone are weak (Helvetica Rounded vs Helvetica showed no significant difference), so the childish read most likely comes from the combination of 4px outlines, 5px hard shadows, big radii, heavy Black weight everywhere and bright flat plates. Fetches that failed: pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov (verification wall), fontsinuse Cyberpunk page (socket hang up), dreadxp (404, snippet only). Several links are search-result pages I read only via the search summary (UXmatters, Discord thread, SlideShare, freeicon, aufaitux, itch.io guides); weigh them lower. I did not name any private tone references and downloaded nothing.

## Games: how teen-rated colourful games build their HUD and menus

- 1. HADES (Supergiant; ESRB T: alcohol reference, blood, mild language, suggestive themes, violence). TYPEFACES: no confirmed UI typeface; Supergiant has not published a font list, so named fonts such as Cinzel or Alegreya are (unconfirmed). One secondary guide says body/UI text is clean and readable, with a more classical, stylised treatment for headers and names, and a custom logo wordmark. SHAPE/OUTLINE: the interface is hand-illustrated art plus set type: ability (boon) cards, menus, dialogue boxes and the codex are described as having ornamental borders, painterly portraits and carefully set type. UI is made by an FX/UI artist (Josh Barnett) with illustrators under the art director. PALETTE: not documented in text sources found (unconfirmed). SLOTS/TIMER: boons are offered as a choice of three (Wikipedia); slot/timer styling not described in text sources found (unconfirmed). WHY NOT CHILDISH: decoration is classical and illustrated, not rounded-plastic; adult content (nudity, mythic drama) is carried by painterly portraits. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/hades-font/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades_(video_game) ; https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=1145360&filters=ratings&cc=us ; https://www.supergiantgames.com/blog/job-opportunity-ui-illustrator-for-hades-ii))
- 2. HI-FI RUSH (Tango Gameworks; ESRB T: fantasy violence, language; PEGI 12). TYPEFACES: none confirmed in text sources (unconfirmed). SHAPE/OUTLINE: cel-shaded 2D look with strong outlines; director John Johanas wanted a 2D look that 'doesn't feel like a Japanese anime' but 'doesn't feel completely like an American made game', citing Jet Set Radio, Viewtiful Joe and Okami; Wikipedia tags it as cel-shaded and graffiti-style. PALETTE: 'colorful, sharp, and clean look'. UI: the team first tried building with no UI at all (producer's suggestion) and leans on beat-synced cues in the world; Wikipedia says critics found combat hard to read when the screen fills with chaos (a legibility warning). WHY NOT CHILDISH: Johanas: 'Our game is ridiculous, it's over the top, and it's about music'; Video Games Chronicle: 'oozing with style and confidence'. Lesson: clean, sharp, confident graphic edges plus very little chrome. Slots/timer: no inventory; HUD details not described in sources found (unconfirmed). ([source](https://news.xbox.com/en-us/2023/10/04/hi-fi-rush-exclusive-oral-history/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hi-Fi_Rush ; https://vice.com/en/article/88qyw5/how-a-designer-known-for-horror-made-the-musical-gaming-triumph-hi-fi-rush ; https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=1817230&filters=ratings,short_description,genres&cc=us))
- 3. PERSONA 5 ROYAL (Atlus; BORDERLINE: ESRB M, PEGI 16, USK 16; kept because it is the best-documented 'stylish adult menu'). TYPEFACES: no named in-game font confirmed; one secondary guide describes heavy condensed sans capitals, often rotated and laid over red shapes, with free look-alikes Anton, Archivo Black, Oswald (look-alikes only, (unconfirmed) as the real fonts). SHAPE: custom-drawn, jagged, cut-paper / ransom-note-like panels; art director Masayoshi Suto reportedly took typographic inspiration from ransom-note record covers (secondary reprint). PALETTE: red, black and white; the hovered item grows larger and gets a splash of blue; battle action buttons coloured to match the controller buttons. HUD: main actions around the active character, party HP/SP bottom-right, enemy name/level top-left; pause menu is a stacked column with a currency readout beside it. WHY NOT CHILDISH: a three-colour limit, aggressive angles, big black/white contrast, confident motion; no rounded friendly plates. Outline/shadow specifics and timer: not covered by sources (unconfirmed). ([source](https://mechanicsofmagic.com/2022/04/23/visual-design-of-games-persona-5/ ; https://segabits.com/?p=54918 ; https://madegooddesigns.com/persona-5-font/ ; https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=1687950&filters=ratings&cc=us))
- 4. VALORANT (Riot; ESRB T stated widely, not re-verified here (unconfirmed)). TYPEFACES: one font guide infers a DIN-Next-like condensed sans for UI and a Tungsten-like condensed face for large numbers/headers; Riot has not published the fonts, so (unconfirmed). SHAPE: Riot's 5.08 lobby redesign (Visual Design Manager Oliver Zumstein): 'we increased the screen's contrast, pushed color values, and simplified our shape language'; 'We had a lot of decoration elements that were pretty but didn't really serve a purpose'. Main-menu tabs became bold text in a vertical list, and the Play button became larger and red (OneEsports coverage). A third-party design-token site claims square corners, a red accent and a -6 degree skewed call-to-action (low reliability, (unconfirmed)). TIMER: round timer 100 s, Spike detonates after 45 s (Wikipedia). WHY NOT CHILDISH: flat, high-contrast, sharp-cornered, one hot red accent, decoration removed on purpose. Slot styling (buy menu) not described in text sources found (unconfirmed). ([source](https://playvalorant.com/en-us/news/game-updates/preview-the-future-of-valorant-s-interface/ ; https://www.oneesports.gg/valorant/valorant-new-ui-patch-5-08/ ; https://madegooddesigns.com/valorant-font/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valorant))
- 5. NEON WHITE (Angel Matrix; ESRB T: fantasy violence, mild blood, mild suggestive themes, language, tobacco; PEGI 12). TYPEFACES: UI font not confirmed; the logo is custom (unconfirmed source). SHAPE: critics describe 'angular aesthetics' (Destructoid), a Dreamcast-era / 1990s game-magazine look (PC Gamer, Giant Bomb), Killer7 and anime influence. PALETTE: 3D levels use hard lines and stark colours with one colour theme per world; character portraits use black, white and one accent colour; Angels appear as cartoon cats (Gaming Trend). SLOTS/TIMER: weapons are 'Soul Cards' held in hand slots, discarded for a movement ability; levels are scored with Bronze/Silver/Gold/Ace medals by completion time (Wikipedia). The card/HUD layout itself is not described in sources found (unconfirmed). WHY NOT CHILDISH: a restricted black/white + one accent palette and hard geometry; speedrun-timer seriousness; the cat Angels are the playful part, the UI is not. ([source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_White ; https://gamingtrend.com/reviews/neon-white-review-bullet-ballet/ ; https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=1533420&filters=ratings&cc=us))
- 6. CULT OF THE LAMB (Massive Monster; Steam shows USK 12, Brazil 14; ESRB not shown in source, (unconfirmed)). TYPEFACES: Fonts In Use (secondary page, it conflicts with another guide that names none) says custom blackletter-inspired title letterforms with slightly rounded thick edges, and menus/dialogue in JAF Lapture Bold + Fira Sans. SHAPE/PALETTE: 'cute-meets-creepy': soft greens, fleece whites, pastel blues and yellows for base-building; high-contrast crimson on stark black for rituals and combat; neon magenta/purple/teal for corruption. WHY NOT CHILDISH: the pastel base is deliberately undercut by blackletter, crimson-on-black ritual screens and occult symbols. CAUTION: reviewers still call the menus 'cozy' and the look 'candy-like', so it only partly passes the 'grown-up' test for this project. Slots/timer: not described (unconfirmed). ([source](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/77885/cult-of-the-lamb-video-game ; https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=10964 ; https://store.steampowered.com/api/appdetails?appids=1313140&filters=ratings&cc=us))
- 7. DEEP ROCK GALACTIC (Ghost Ship Games; USK 16: drastic violence, alcohol/tobacco, profanity; ESRB/PEGI not shown in the source, (unconfirmed)). TYPEFACES/UI: no text source found names the UI font or describes the HUD (unconfirmed). ## Item icons

- GAP, read first: no primary source (studio talk, style guide, postmortem) was found that says a given icon style reads grown-up or childish in a named teen/adult game. Searches for Borderlands, Cyberpunk 2077, Deep Rock Galactic, Hades, Diablo IV, TF2, Overwatch and Dota 2 icon pipelines returned only secondary material. The grown-up/childish verdicts below are therefore reasoning from the general craft sources, marked (inference), and need a visual test in the wave rather than trust. ([source](https://www.pcgamer.com/uk/a-spot-of-cel-shading-turns-cyberpunk-2077-into-a-convincing-borderlands-sequel))
- Toy-like soft 3D is tied to childhood by its own advocates: claymorphism is described as resembling stuffed toys, balloons and claymation, with clay evoking childhood and games, and design kits list kids/education and friendly consumer apps as its fit. (inference) Puffy, soft-plastic, glossy-bubble icons will push toward 'game for kids'; the aliens' vinyl-toy look is fine on the characters, but the item icons should not copy that softness. ([source](https://dev.to/perpetio/claymorphism-what-it-is-and-why-this-style-is-becoming-a-top-2022-ui-trend-52ll))
- Cult of the Lamb's art director said the directors kept pushing his drawings from too cute toward more evil/monstrous and settled on something more grounded in reality; the same game was praised by another studio for a clean, almost icon-like look. This is a documented example of a colourful teen/adult game actively pulling away from cute, by adding grounded weight and edge rather than by changing palette. ([source](https://www.dreadxp.com/?p=27832))
- Diablo IV's art director states the goal as 'not realism, something that feels like a painting', with oil-painting and old-master references, and a worry about losing the hand-crafted feel. This supports the painted/painterly-render route as the premium-adult register, though the interview is about the whole game, not icons. ([source](https://gamespot.com/articles/diablo-4s-art-direction-emphasizes-hand-crafted-details-plenty-of-customization-and-just-enough-realism/1100-6493411/))
- Four candidate treatments, with my assessment (all inference except where cited). (1) Rendered 3D icons of real-looking objects: best match for the 'realistic, well-made present-day home' brief and the product-object feel of beer, pizza, cans; premium if lit hard-and-clean with real materials, childish if rounded, puffy and saturated. The 0 A.D. team and Polycount commenters describe the rendered-then-painted-over hybrid as a normal route to readable icons. (2) Painted icons: richest, reads adult when value-driven and slightly rough; costly to keep coherent without a human painter. (3) Flat icons with an ink line: closest to the current sticker look, so highest risk of staying 'kids'; one inventory-icon write-up found light icons need a dark outline at small size. (4) Photographic product-shot icons: reads modern/fashionable and deadpan, fits the absurd-meets-real comedy of real pizza and beer cans next to alien items; risk is clutter and loss of the game's colour system at 48 px unless backgrounds are removed and contrast is lifted. ([source](https://play0ad.com/?p=2755))
- One game team that tried rendered 3D icons found them unpolished and not fitting a cartoony, simply shaded game; they painted concepts and vectorised instead, and found realistic material shading clashed with simple shading. Lesson: the icon style must match the shading level of the surrounding game art, not just be 'good'. For this game, items are real-world objects and the house is realistic, so realistic material shading is the match, the opposite of that team's case. (Their game's look is not ours.) ([source](https://www.chasing-carrots.com/how-to-create-inventory-icons/))
- Silhouette is the primary test: fill the icon solid black; if items still differ by shape, the set reads fast. Differentiate by overall shape first, colour and fine detail second. Design at the largest size, judge at the smallest. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=5494))
- Coherence rules that recur across sources: one rendering style per set with matching line weight, detail level, lighting direction and perspective; same canvas and identical optical padding baked into every export; mixing flat and heavily rendered icons makes the set look broken; judge icons in a grid against neighbours and over the real UI background; similar visual weight (a thin icon beside a heavy one breaks rhythm). ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=5494))
- Small-slot findings: 0 A.D. authors icons at 128 px or larger and requires them to stay recognisable at 40x40 by using high contrast and exaggerated shapes. An inventory-icon write-up found a white outline turned grainy when scaled down; a dark outline was needed for light icons on bright backgrounds; with no outline it was hard to find backgrounds with enough contrast; slightly warping an object's shape gave a more characteristic look; use plate colour for the item category, not to identify single items; map item colours to the game palette instead of realistic colours; and test several styles on one simple object, then the full range of item types. ([source](https://www.chasing-carrots.com/how-to-create-inventory-icons/))
- Rarity or category should be shown by frame colour or shape, backed by a shape cue so colour-blind players are served; check contrast against all possible plate colours early. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=5494))
- Implication for 48 to 96 px HUD slots (inference): at 48 px expect about 5 to 7 readable shapes in the icon; drop texture, fine labels, and brand-like print; keep one dominant silhouette, one hard key light with one rim, and a 2 to 3 px safe margin; give every slot the same dark, matte, near-flat plate (the deep purple already liked) so the plate carries the style and the icon only carries the object. A dark-edge or thin dark rim on the item (not the 4 px sticker outline) keeps light items such as pizza box, cream cans and cheese separate from the plate. Author at 512 or 1024, downsample, and review at 48 first. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=5494))
- Image-model route, one sheet: generating the whole set as a single image makes the items share a style by construction; the sheet can then be reused as a style reference to extend the set later, and the new items pick up palette and outline treatment (vendor claim, SpriteCook). A Claude Code skill template also recommends one grid call, cut apart client-side, and says the OpenAI model is the one worth using when transparent PNGs are needed in one shot. ([source](https://www.spritecook.ai/game-icon-generator))
- OpenAI's prompting guide: label sections explicitly ('Panel 1:', 'Panel 2:') or use short labeled segments; specify grid structure such as '4 equal-sized panels'; ask for 'a clean, flat visual system with consistent icon style'; list every required component and what to exclude; repeat a 'preserve' list on every iteration to reduce drift; refer to input images by index and description ('Image 1: ... Image 2: style reference'); use single-change follow-ups ('change only X, keep everything else the same'). It says camera and lens wording is 'interpreted loosely' so use it for overall look, and that the word 'photorealistic' engages the photorealistic mode. The guide itself has no sprite-sheet section. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.md))
- Transparent background: set background=transparent with output_format png or webp (JPEG cannot hold alpha); ask for an isolated subject on a fully transparent background and exclude scenery, backdrops, checkerboards and shadows; keep the alpha channel (converting RGBA to RGB discards it); for edits say explicitly to preserve the transparent background in each prompt. The guide labels this a preview feature of gpt-image-2. A forum user warned that a checkerboard seen in output may be painted pixels and not real alpha, so check the alpha channel, not the preview. Reports differ on whether the edit endpoint supports transparency, so test it. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.md))
- Fallback if alpha is unreliable on a sheet: a flat chroma-key background (one tool uses #00ff00 and needs a tolerance setting so green pixels in the art are not eaten). Caution for this game: mint/teal alien skin and the alien green main-action colour sit close to green, so a magenta or other off-palette key colour is safer than green. (inference) ([source](https://www.spritecook.ai/game-icon-generator))
- Sizes in the current API: 1024x1024, 1536x1024, 1024x1536 are recommended; newer models accept custom sizes in multiples of 16, ratio up to 3:1, edge up to 3840, and resolutions above 2560x1440 are experimental. A 2x3 or 3x4 sheet in 1536x1024 gives cells of roughly 512 px or 384 px, enough for 96 px slots. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/image-generation))
- Style-anchor technique: take one approved icon (or the first sheet), pass it as the reference image in later calls ('Image 1 is the style reference: match its material, light, camera and outline exactly; draw only the items listed'), and repeat the fixed style paragraph word for word on every call. A general glossary describes this as applying an extracted style as a conditioning signal across the rest of the set (not GPT-specific). ([source](https://insertchat.com/glossary/icon-generation))
- Fixed words to put in one reusable style paragraph (inference from the sources above): camera (orthographic-looking, 3/4 view from slightly above, same angle, same scale, item filling about 80 percent of its cell), light (one soft key from upper left, one cool rim light from lower right, no cast shadow, no ground), material (real-world product materials: enamel, aluminium, glossy plastic, cardboard), outline (none, or a thin dark edge), plate (none, because the plate is added in the UI), background (transparent), exclusions (no text, no logos, no labels, no borders, no grid lines, no characters, no hands). Vary one item name per cell, not the style words. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.md))
- Other published tips: vary one prompt element at a time and keep the best parts; design for small size first and test every version as a thumbnail; fix the prompt template (main element, lighting, composition, palette); lock camera angle, palette, outline weight, proportions and readability rules before generating related assets; vendors suggest a custom LoRA on reference art for consistency, which is not available in the OpenAI API route. ([source](https://www.3daistudio.com/blog/create-professional-game-icons-using-ai-guide))
- Cost against the $3 cap: OpenAI lists image output at $30 per million tokens for gpt-image-2 ($40 for gpt-image-1, $8 for gpt-image-1-mini), with batch at half price. Per-image figures reported by several third parties for 1024x1024 are about $0.006 low, $0.053 medium, $0.211 high; one measured high-quality run cost about $0.26, and reference images add input cost. Practical plan: about 6 to 8 exploratory sheets at medium (about $0.4), then 3 to 6 final high sheets (up to about $1.5), leaving margin for 1536x1024 sheets costing more than square ones (unconfirmed). Not an official calculator; verify with one test call and read the billed usage. ([source](https://blog.segmind.com/ai-image-gen-pricing-compared-nano-banana-2-vs-seedream-5-0-vs-gpt-image-2-2026/))
- Model naming warning: OpenAI's own docs now describe gpt-image-2 and 2.5 variants, and the fetched guide pages did not describe gpt-image-1 transparency or the input_fidelity parameter consistently (the cookbook says input_fidelity does not work for gpt-image-2 but its examples pass it). Confirm the model id the account can call and test transparency and reference-image behaviour on one cheap low-quality call before the paid sheets. ([source](https://developers.openai.com/cookbook/examples/multimodal/image-gen-models-prompting-guide.md))
- Recommendation for the redesign wave (inference, to be tested): treat 'grown-up' as three levers, not a style name. (a) Realistic item rendering with hard-ish light and real materials instead of rounded candy shapes; (b) one matte dark plate per slot in the existing deep purple with a thin dark or 1 to 2 px edge instead of the 4 px mint-ink sticker outline and hard 5 px shadow; (c) palette discipline: mint and alien green kept for the main action and coral for alarm, items allowed to bring their real colours but with saturation held down. Test two treatments on the same 6 items (beer can, pizza slice, energy drink, bucket, pool float, key) as a 3 by 2 sheet: 'clean product render' and 'painted-over render', each shown at 96, 64 and 48 px on the real plate, with the silhouette fill test next to it. ([source](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=5494))
